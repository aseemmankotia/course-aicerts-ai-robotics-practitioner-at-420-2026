## Chapter 8: Applied Robotics: Practical Use-Cases — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | AGV vs AMR: core navigation difference? | AGV = fixed guidepath (magnetic tape, wire, floor markers), no obstacle rerouting. AMR = SLAM-based dynamic mapping, reroutes around obstacles in real time. Exam tip: dynamic/unpredictable environment = AMR. |
| 2 | Industrial robot vs cobot: key safety design difference? | Industrial robots rely on fixed guarding/fencing to exclude humans (high speed/payload). Cobots use power and force limiting (PFL) + speed and separation monitoring (SSM) to work safely near humans without full fencing. |
| 3 | Which standard defines biomechanical contact force/pressure limits for cobot PFL configuration? | ISO/TS 15066 (extends ISO 10218). Defines per-body-region force/pressure thresholds. Exam tip: don't confuse with ISO 9001 (quality) or IEC 61508 (general functional safety). |
| 4 | Typical cobot payload range vs industrial robot payload range? | Cobots: roughly 3kg–20kg (some models up to ~35kg). Industrial robots: can exceed 1,000kg depending on class. Exam tip: high payload + high speed scenario = industrial robot. |
| 5 | What coordinates task allocation and traffic management across a fleet of AMRs? | A Fleet Management System (FMS) — handles task assignment, traffic/intersection management, and charging scheduling across multiple AMRs operating in shared space. |
| 6 | What is the standard architecture pattern for surgical assist robots? | Master-slave teleoperation: surgeon controls instruments via a console; system applies tremor filtering and motion scaling. Not autonomous — exam distractor is 'fully autonomous surgery'. |
| 7 | UV-C disinfection robots: mandatory safety feature in occupied spaces? | Human-presence/occupancy detection that automatically disables UV emission when a person is nearby, since UV-C exposure (~254nm wavelength) is hazardous to skin/eyes. |
| 8 | Typical fixed-path industrial palletizing cell architecture components? | Fixed-base high-payload arm + vision/sensor system + conveyor integration + perimeter guarding/light curtains. Used when no humans are in the cell during operation. |
| 9 | ROI advantage of cobots vs industrial robot cells? | Cobots have lower deployment cost and faster redeployment (no fencing, quick changeover between tasks), making ROI timelines shorter for small/variable-batch production vs fixed industrial cells. |
| 10 | Service robots (hospitality/retail) vs industrial robots: design focus? | Service robots prioritize human interaction (voice/UI, navigation in public unstructured spaces, lower payload). Industrial robots prioritize precision, payload, and repeatability in structured, human-excluded environments. |
| 11 | What safety performance level (per ISO 13849) is typically required for cobot safety-rated stop functions? | Performance Level d (PLd) or e (PLe), often achieved via Category 3 or 4 safety circuits — tested as the control-system reliability requirement behind cobot safety functions. |
| 12 | Last-mile hospital delivery robot (medication between floors) — which robot class and why? | AMR/service robot with elevator and building-system integration — needs dynamic navigation through occupied, variable hallways and automated elevator calls; AGVs can't adapt to unplanned obstacles. |
