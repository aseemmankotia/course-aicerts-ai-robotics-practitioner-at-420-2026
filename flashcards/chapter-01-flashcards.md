## Chapter 1: Robotics and AI Foundations — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Degrees of Freedom (DOF) definition | Number of independent parameters needed to fully define a system's position/configuration in space. Exam tip: a standard industrial arm is often 6 DOF (matches full 6D pose: x,y,z + roll,pitch,yaw). |
| 2 | Forward kinematics vs Inverse kinematics | Forward: joint angles → end-effector position (direct calculation). Inverse: end-effector position → joint angles (often multiple solutions, computationally harder). Exam tip: inverse kinematics is used when the robot must 'reach' a target. |
| 3 | Proprioceptive vs Exteroceptive sensors | Proprioceptive = measures internal robot state (encoders, IMU, joint position sensors). Exteroceptive = measures external environment (camera, lidar, ultrasonic). Exam tip: IMU and gyroscope are proprioceptive even though they seem environment-related. |
| 4 | Electric vs Hydraulic vs Pneumatic actuators | Electric: precise, clean, moderate force — most common in modern robots. Hydraulic: highest force density, used for heavy industrial loads, high maintenance. Pneumatic: fast, lightweight, lower precision due to air compressibility. Exam tip: match actuator choice to the force/precision requirement in the scenario. |
| 5 | Perception-Plan-Act (Sense-Think-Act) loop | Perception (sense environment) → Plan (decide action using world model) → Act (execute via actuators). Exam tip: this is the foundational control loop directly tested on the exam. |
| 6 | Robot taxonomy: Manipulator vs Mobile robot vs Humanoid | Manipulator: fixed-base arm (e.g., welding robot). Mobile robot: moves through the environment (AGV, drone). Humanoid: human-like form combining mobility and manipulation. Exam tip: classify by base mobility plus form factor. |
| 7 | How AI augments classical robotics control | AI (ML/RL) adapts or tunes classical control methods (PID, kinematics) using learned models instead of fixed equations — e.g., RL-tuned gains, vision-based perception feeding a planner. Exam tip: classical control = fixed math; AI = adaptive/learned layer on top. |
| 8 | Example proprioceptive sensors | Joint encoder, IMU, gyroscope, joint force/torque sensor. Exam tip: anything measuring the robot's OWN internal state is proprioceptive. |
| 9 | Example exteroceptive sensors | Camera, LiDAR, ultrasonic sensor, infrared proximity sensor. Exam tip: anything measuring the EXTERNAL environment is exteroceptive. |
| 10 | Cost/complexity tradeoff: Hydraulic actuators | Highest force output, but highest maintenance cost (fluid leaks, pumps, seals) and bulkiest design. Exam tip: choose hydraulic only when the scenario emphasizes heavy force/load, not precision or cleanliness. |
| 11 | Cost/complexity tradeoff: Pneumatic actuators | Low cost, fast response, lightweight — but air compressibility reduces positional precision and load-holding ability. Exam tip: good for simple binary actions (grip/release), not precise continuous positioning. |
| 12 | Robotics vs AI definitional distinction | Robotics = engineering discipline combining perception, control, and actuation in physical systems. AI = algorithms for intelligent decision-making, not necessarily embodied. Exam tip: AI can exist without a robot body; robotics typically requires physical actuation. |
